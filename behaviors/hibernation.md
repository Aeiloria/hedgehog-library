## Essence

Hibernation is **controlled torpor**, not ordinary sleep. For wild European hedgehogs (*Erinaceus europaeus*) metabolism nearly stands still so the animal can survive cold winters when insect prey vanishes.

> Floor first. Winter is the longest floor.

In this library, hibernation maps to the **deep sleep vault** — architecture and care that protect energy, not force activity.

---

## Phases (wild biology)

### 1. Preparation (autumn)

- **White fat** — primary fuel for the long torpor  
- **Brown fat** — shoulders / spine; rapid heat on arousal  
- **Hibernaculum** — dry leaves, twigs, grass, moss under log piles, sheds, dense brush, compost heaps

### 2. Torpor physiology (often ambient ~<10°C)

| Function | Active | Torpor |
|----------|--------|--------|
| Body temp | ~35°C | ~1–4°C |
| Heart rate | 190–280 bpm | 20–50 bpm |
| Respiration | 25–50 / min | ~1 breath every few minutes |
| Metabolism | high | reduced >90% |

### 3. Cycle (not one continuous nap)

- Typical season: Oct/Nov → Mar/Apr  
- Periodic arousals every few weeks (costly — underweight animals may not survive repeats)  
- Nest switching on milder days if damp, unsafe, or disturbed  

### 4. Emergence (spring)

- Brown-fat thermogenesis warms organs  
- Weight loss often 20–40% — urgent food + water on waking  

---

## Care Ecology warnings

1. **Pet African pygmy hedgehogs do not hibernate.** Cold-induced torpor in captivity is a medical emergency — gentle warming, not “let them sleep it out.”  
2. **Disturbance burns fat.** Waking a wild hibernator for curiosity can kill them before spring.  
3. **Consent of the vault.** The sleep vault is a Threshold: do not enter without need.
